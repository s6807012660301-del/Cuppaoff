Suggested Site Structure

1. Hero
Tagline + one strong sentence. Something like: "The cup you don't throw away — you eat it." CTA: "Shop Molds" / "Join the Movement."

2. The Problem
Quick stats on disposable cup waste (billions of cups landfilled yearly, non-recyclable coatings, etc.) — this sets up why Cuppa exists before you sell anything.

3. What We Offer Today (Year 1)
Molds + ingredients + a "how to make your own edible cup" guide/tutorial. Present this as a DIY kit — empowering, not just a product.

4. Our Journey / Roadmap
This is where your 1 → 1.5 → 2-5 year plan goes, framed as a mission timeline rather than a corporate roadmap:

Phase 1 (Now): Molds + ingredients — make your own edible cup at home.
Phase 1.5: Cuppa's own recipe cups launch — buy the cup pre-made, or keep making your own.
Phase 2-5: Flavors expand (Original, Strawberry, Oat...) designed to pair with yogurt or protein shakes — built for the fitness and diet-conscious community.

5. Save the World With Us
Your sustainability pitch — zero waste, compostable/edible = literally nothing to throw away, reduces plastic-lined cup waste, etc. This can be its own emotional section with the impact-counter animation.

6. Future Vision (Teaser)
A "coming soon" preview of flavors + the fitness/diet angle — builds anticipation even before Phase 2 launches.

7. Community / Waitlist
Email signup: "Be the first to try Cuppa flavors" — start building your fitness/diet audience now, even in Year 1.

8. Footer
Story/About, contact, social.

On the "Save the World" Angle

Frame it around three concrete pillars rather than vague eco-language:

Zero waste — nothing to throw away, nothing sitting in a landfill for centuries.
No plastic lining — most "paper" cups aren't recyclable because of plastic coating; edible cups sidestep that entirely.
Circular by design — you eat the packaging, so the "waste stream" is literally your stomach.
Colorful Brand Theme

Overall vibe: vibrant, fun, "treat yourself" energy — closer to a boba/dessert brand than a traditional coffee shop. This actually works well with your roadmap since you're heading toward fruity flavors (strawberry, oat) and a fitness/lifestyle audience.

Color palette (multi-color but controlled):

Primary base: cream or soft white background (keeps it from feeling chaotic)
Coffee accent: rich brown/espresso for product realism and text
Flavor colors as brand colors — this is the key trick: let your future flavors define your palette now, so it grows naturally with your roadmap
Strawberry → coral pink/red
Oat → warm golden yellow
Original → caramel/tan
Add a fresh mint or sage green for the "eco/sustainability" sections
Use these as accent blocks, buttons, icons, and section backgrounds — not all at once, but rotating by section so each part of the site feels distinct

Typography: keep one bold, rounded, friendly sans-serif (like a candy/snack brand font) — colorful backgrounds need calm, confident type or it gets messy.

Shapes & graphics: soft blobby shapes, rounded cup illustrations, confetti-like dot patterns, hand-drawn style icons (steam swirls, leaf icons, droplet icons) — gives it a fun, modern startup feel rather than corporate.

How Color Maps to Your Roadmap (nice storytelling trick)
Year 1 section: mostly caramel/brown tones (the "plain" DIY mold stage)
1.5 year section: introduce your first brand color pop (maybe golden yellow) as "our own recipe" launches
2-5 year section: full color palette unlocks — pink, yellow, green all appear together as flavors launch → visually shows growth/expansion as the user scrolls through your roadmap

This way the site itself gets more colorful as you scroll through the timeline, mirroring your actual product evolution. It's a strong visual metaphor and gives you a reason for the colorful theme beyond "just looks fun."
